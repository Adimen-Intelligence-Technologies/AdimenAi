# E-invoicing 2026: what you need to change and why almost nobody knows

**TL;DR.** Invoicing will shift from optional to a **progressive obligation** in Spain by 2026. Whether you're a company or freelancer, you need to know who is required, what type of invoice to issue, and what software you can use without overhauling everything overnight. The key: **VeriFactu** (real-time verification) started in 2025 with large companies and will extend to SMEs. It's not "bureaucracy": it's a change in how money is controlled.

## Are you required to invoice electronically?

Not everyone is. The rule is simple:

- **Large companies (>€20M annual turnover):** yes, since 2025.
- **Medium companies (€2M–20M):** yes, but phased in (2025–2027).
- **Small SMEs (<€2M):** not yet required, but the mandate is on the way.
- **Freelancers:** **no**, not for now. Nobody knows if it will tighten.

What is certain: if **your clients are large companies**, they will **probably demand e-invoicing already**. That's why, even if you're not mandated by law, **getting ready avoids supply chain breaks.**

## What is VeriFactu?

**Real-time verification** (VeriFactu) is the system Tax Agency uses to monitor that issued and accepted invoices are **linked to an electronic record book** that's reported in real time. In other words:

- The Tax Agency sees **almost instantly** what you invoice.
- Each invoice must be in an **electronic record book**.
- The software used **must be certified** to comply.

This applies to **both your issued invoices and received invoices**.

## What type of invoice should you issue?

Depends on who asks:

| Client | Invoice type |
|---|---|
| Freelancer or small company | Regular invoice (email, PDF) |
| Medium company under VeriFactu | Specific electronic invoice |
| Public administration | Electronic invoice (always required) |
| Large company | Electronic invoice (they also demand VeriFactu from suppliers) |

Not every client asks for the same thing. That's why your software must **emit multiple invoice types** without switching systems.

## Mistakes that can get you fined

1. **Issuing paper invoices to a company that requires electronic ones.** That's a problem for you and the client: it doesn't match.
2. **Using software without VeriFactu certification.** If the client needs it and you can't provide it, you lose the deal.
3. **Missing electronic record books.** Direct AEAT penalty.
4. **Invoicing with fake or manipulated VAT.** AEAT already detects anomalies in real time.

## What software can you use?

You don't need to overhaul your system. What you need is **software that can:

- **Issue invoices** in multiple formats (PDF, email, electronic).
- **Has VeriFactu certification** (the program states it when installed).
- **Integrates with your POS or ERP**.
- **Generates record books automatically**.

## How not to break your supply chain

The worst consequence of not being ready isn't the fine: it's that a large client **stops ordering from you**. Real-time verification forces supply chains to monitor suppliers.

**Concrete actions:**

1. Ask your top 3 clients: what invoice format do you demand today?
2. Pass that info to your accountant or current software.
3. Request an e-invoicing demo before changing anything.
4. Keep normal invoicing (PDF, email) for clients that don't require it.

Not everyone upgrades at once. **E-invoicing will roll out progressively**, like VAT did. Adapt now and avoid surprises.

## And if I'm a freelancer? Do I need to change anything?

Not yet, officially. But:

- If you work with large companies, many already demand e-invoicing.
- Some autonomous communities (Andalusia, Madrid) are already testing.
- The **digital economy bill** circulating in 2026 includes freelancers, though not yet law.

Best advice: **talk to your accountant.** Many are already updating systems just in case.

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

**When is e-invoicing mandatory in Spain?**
For large companies (>€20M turnover) since 2025. Phased for medium SMEs by 2026–2027. For small SMEs, no date yet, but the path is set.

**And for freelancers?**
Not mandatory yet. But if you work with large companies, many already demand it.

**What if I don't change my software?**
If not required by law, nothing happens immediately. But you lose clients who do need e-invoicing.

**Can I invoice by email to a large company?**
Yes, if the client accepts. What you **cannot** do is invoice on paper if the client requires electronic. That's a broken relationship.

**What happens if I don't adapt?**
Nothing immediately if you're not mandated. But you'll lose clients who do require e-invoicing.

**Is it expensive to adapt?**
Depends on software. From €30/month (basic tools) to €150–250/month (enterprise VeriFactu solutions).

**Does VeriFactu affect freelancers?**
Not yet. Current regulations focus on large companies and supply chains.

**Can AI issue invoices?**
AI doesn't issue invoices. But it can help validate data, read supplier invoice PDFs, and classify them.

## In summary

- You're not required today if you're a small SME or freelancer.
- You should be **ready** if your clients demand it.
- Ask your clients what format they need.
- Don't overhaul everything: use software that supports multiple formats.
- Talk to your accountant. They're already adapting.
